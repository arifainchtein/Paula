Short answer: **yes, it can work if you use SCL (not SDA) as DE**, with two catches. Here's what I found from Paula.ino and the Rosie RS485 Adapter board (MAX485E, terminals TX / DE / RX).

**Why SCL and not SDA**
- The I2C bus has pull-ups, so when it's idle both lines sit HIGH. On a MAX485, DE high means "transmit". So whenever I2C is idle, the adapter is transmitting and can't hear the sensor.
- To receive the sensor's reply, the firmware has to hold DE **LOW**. Holding SCL low just freezes the I2C bus (every chip waits for the clock), which is harmless. Holding SDA low while SCL is high is an I2C START condition, so the chips would think a transaction had begun. SCL is the clean choice.

**Catch 1: while the soil test runs, nothing else on I2C works.** Paula would call `Wire.end()`, take GPIO 22 as a plain output for DE, and call `Wire.begin()` again when the test stops. That's the same "borrow the pin during the test" pattern `ReadClipsal` already uses for 18/33. Things affected during the test:
- the OLED
- RTC reads (the 1 Hz tick on pin 4 keeps running, since that's a separate pin)
- the ADS1115, SHT3x and INA228

For a bench test button that's fine. It wouldn't suit a deployed unit that has to keep the display and RTC going.

**Catch 2: the board wiring needs checking before trusting it.** Two things I couldn't confirm from the files:
- That the I2C screw terminal on Paula/Daffodil is GPIO 22 straight through, or through a BSS138 shifter. Either works, since a pushed GPIO passes through a BSS138 fine.
- Whether the adapter has a pull-down on DE. With the I2C pull-up winning, DE would sit high at idle, so the adapter keeps driving A/B whenever the test isn't running. That's harmless with only the soil probe on the bus.

**Pin plan**
- **18 → TX** (the adapter's DI), through the existing 2N7002 shifter. A pushed output at 9600 baud gets through it.
- **33 → RX** (the adapter's RO, which is 5 V), as the second Clipsal ultrasonic already does on UART1.
- **SCL (22) → DE**: HIGH while sending the Modbus request, then LOW right after `flush()` to listen.

The Modbus side would reuse `querySoilSensor()` from `rs485SensorTest.ino`: address 2, 9600 8N1, function 0x03, registers 0–7 (temperature, moisture, EC, salt, N, P, K, pH).

**The yellow "Test Soil Sensor" button** would need:
- **Paula:** new `ReadSoil` / `StopSoil` serial commands, firmware version 13, and an entry in the Help list.
- **PaulaDeployer:** a yellow "Test Soil Sensor" button next to the other Clipsal tests. It would show the 8 readings and a reminder that the OLED/RTC go quiet during the test and the 18/33 jumper must be on DIGITAL.

Should I go ahead and write both? I'd edit them and you'd compile and flash.

Context for using the Clipsall RS485 board (HardwareDesign/ClipsallJunctionBoxWaterTrough_RS485, MAX485E) with Daffodil.
This was diagnosed and measured on Gloria on 2026-11-26.

The problem
- The Clipsall RS485 board was designed for Rosie/Wally: its RX output goes to a bare ESP32 input (GPIO39, no pull-up).
  On the board, RO (MAX485 pin 1) reaches the J2 RX terminal through a divider: R2 10k from RO to RX, R3 20k from RX to GND.
  That gives 5 V -> about 3.3 V, which is right for a bare GPIO.
- Daffodil Rev 6+ (and the Gloria rev 1 shield, which copied it) feeds its sensor terminals on GPIO 18/33/32 through a
  2N7002 level shifter: 4.7k to V33 on the GPIO side, 10k to V50 on the terminal side (Daffodil.ino: Q1 R5/R7 on 33,
  Q2 R6/R8 on 32). That 10k to V50 fights the divider, so when RO pulls low, RX only gets down to about 2.0-2.4 V and the
  ESP32 never sees a low. The UART receives nothing.
- Measured on Gloria (A tied to GND, B tied to V50 so RO is forced low): RO = 7 mV, RX = 2.4 V.
- What it looks like: no reply from any sensor and no garbled bytes. It isn't A/B, termination or baud. The transceiver
  hears its own requests (~RE is tied to GND), but none of that echo reaches the ESP32 either.

The fix (confirmed on Gloria: echo on 55 of 56 requests, and the 7-in-1 soil probe was found)
- Bridge R2, so RO goes straight to RX. Either put a solder blob across R2, or remove R2 and bridge its pads with a short
  wire. If the pads lift, solder a wire from MAX485 pin 1 to the back of J2 pin 1 instead. Leave R3 (20k) in place.
  The 0-5 V swing is fine for Daffodil's shifter, which is made for 5 V on the terminal side.
- Check with the meter: MAX485 pin 1 to J2 pin 1 should read about 0 ohm. J2 pin 1 to GND should read about 20k (R3),
  not 0.
- Check with power on: tie A to GND and B to V50. RX should now read under 0.5 V.
- Don't bridge R2 on a board that will go to Rosie/Wally (bare GPIO39). There the divider is needed.

DE (milder version of the same problem)
- The Clipsall's R1 is a 10k pull-down on DE. Against Daffodil's 10k pull-up to V50, DE only reaches about 2.5 V when
  driven high. That's above the MAX485E's 2.0 V minimum, so it works, but with little margin. If transmitting is flaky,
  remove R1.
- Side effect: until the firmware drives DE low, the pull-up holds DE at about 2.5 V, so the transmitter is on during boot.
  That doesn't harm anything.

Wiring (Clipsall J2: pin 1 RX, pin 2 DE, pin 3 TX)
- RX -> the Daffodil terminal of the GPIO used as UART RX. DE -> a terminal GPIO driven HIGH to transmit.
  TX -> the UART TX GPIO. V50 and GND to J5/J3. Sensor A/B to J4. J6 = 120 ohm termination
  (only for long cables, one at each end).
- Firmware: half-duplex Modbus RTU. Because ~RE is tied low, every request comes back as an echo before the reply, so
  strip it. DigitalStablesEsp32Lib's ModbusSensorBus (shared by Gloria/Paula2) does this already and counts it:
  GetSensors shows "own echo on X of Y requests". 0 of Y means the RX path, which is this problem.
  It also needs about 60 ms between requests: the 7-in-1 soil probe ignored a request sent 20 ms after its previous reply.

Next PCB revision: make the RX path selectable (a solder jumper across R2, and R3 optional), marked "3.3 V direct GPIO"
vs "5 V shifted input", and make the DE pull-down optional. One board then works with Rosie, Daffodil and Gloria.
